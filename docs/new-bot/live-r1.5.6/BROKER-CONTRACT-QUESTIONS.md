# TIDE execution: provider questions and offline integration plan

**Status: review only.** Do not install this branch. No account, host, broker endpoint or live order was accessed. This document is a draft for the project team to use if it obtains an authorized provider response; it has not been sent to provider support.

## Current behavior to preserve

The reviewed planner submits one entry at the strategy's chosen price/type and size, protects the filled position at its absolute structural stop, uses the strategy's TP1 allocation and TP2 for the rest, and after TP1 moves the runner's stop to break-even plus one tick, followed by the existing tighter-only ladder. A one-contract trade has no TP1 order: its TP1 price is a break-even trigger. Session/manual flatten remains available. The existing money, timing, entry and HTF settings remain unchanged. These are behavior to demonstrate, not claims about the account's current configuration or live results.

## Exact provider questions

Please answer each question for **the Gateway API and the account's actual bracket mode**, with documentation or a written provider guarantee. A platform description or a single successful simulation is insufficient to establish concurrency behavior.

1. **OCO, partials and races:** In Auto-OCO mode, if a target and its paired stop are triggered nearly simultaneously, can both execute or can the account reverse? If either leg partially fills, is the sibling's remaining quantity reduced atomically before either remaining leg can execute? What happens if the sibling cancel is delayed or rejected?
2. **Parent and child identity:** If an entry partially fills, when do its protective children become active, at what quantities, and what durable fields associate the exact entry, stop and target in order history and real-time events? Are those relationships preserved across reconnects and corrections? Can multiple take-profit quantities be attached to one parent entry, each with correct corresponding protection, without splitting that entry into multiple submissions?
3. **Absolute prices and amendments:** Are stop and target bracket tick offsets anchored to requested entry price, each fill, average fill or another price? Can an absolute structural stop and distinct TP1/TP2 prices be set and retained under slippage and partial fills? Does `/Order/modify` atomically change the existing child order's quantity and/or price with respect to fills, retain its identity and OCO linkage, and avoid a window with zero or two working stops? What is the exact behavior when modification is rejected, pending or its response is lost?
4. **Close versus working orders:** Do `/Position/closeContract` and `/Position/partialCloseContract` atomically limit their execution to the original open side and quantity when a stop/target fills concurrently, including a delayed close request after the original position is flat or reversed? Do they cancel or resize linked and unlinked orders, and when? Is a close uniquely identifiable in order/trade history after a lost response? Is retry idempotent?
5. **Cancellation and fill finality:** After a cancel request is accepted and exact order history reports `Cancelled` or `Expired`, what authoritative event or watermark establishes that all fills preceding cancellation are reflected and cannot later change? How do late/corrected trade events affect the cumulative fill quantity? If no such guarantee exists, what supported primitive provides a safe transition to replacement or close?
6. **Tags and history:** What are the `customTag` length and character limits, uniqueness lifetime, persistence, and lookup guarantees? Does a duplicate-tag rejection identify the original order? What are the retention, pagination, timestamp and eventual-consistency guarantees of Order/search, including rejected/pending and child orders? An empty or incomplete search is not proof that a request will never arrive.
7. **Mode and compatibility:** How can the API read and verify Auto-OCO versus Position Brackets mode and the relevant account eligibility without changing settings? Confirm supported account types and whether all required order, modify, cancel and close actions are available. The existing runtime adapter's accepted response shape and tag forwarding also need verification.

Sources: [ProjectX order placement and bracket modes](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/order/order-place/), [cancel](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/order/order-cancel/), [modify](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/order/order-modify/), [partial close](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/positions/close-positions-partial/), [order search](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/order/order-search/), [realtime events](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/realtime/), and [TopstepX OCO description](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/14434175-topstepx).

## Controlled offline integration plan

1. **Freeze the contract.** Record provider answers, their scope, date and version. For every required property record `confirmed`, `not supported`, or `unknown` with an exact citation. Unknown or unsupported behavior blocks activation of the corresponding adapter path. Do not expose an end-user switch that simply asserts a guarantee. Do not change account settings.
2. **Build a fake provider with explicit capabilities.** Implement separate reference models for provider-linked OCO, cancellation, amendment, close, partial fills, delayed visibility and corrections. The fake model must be configurable to violate each unconfirmed assumption; a model in which OCO is assumed atomic is a hypothesis, not proof about the provider. Use it only offline.
3. **Preserve strategy semantics.** Run synthetic long and short inputs for one- and multi-contract entries, partial parent fills, entry slippage, structural stops, TP1/TP2, break-even and ladder changes, and session/manual flatten. Compare the proposed adapter's order count, entry type/quantity/price, absolute stop, target allocations, and strategy events against the pinned planner. A split-entry design changes order timing and possible fills; it is a different execution design until independently proven acceptable, not an automatic equivalence.
4. **Enumerate adverse schedules.** Against the actual proposed adapter, inject TP1 and stop in both orders before polling, partially filled targets/stops, rejected or pending cancels/amendments/closes, out-of-order and duplicate events, stale snapshots, foreign/missing identities, tag collision, late acceptance, missing replies, and process death at every durable-write/network boundary. Assert real fake-broker signed inventory, all working orders, protection state and durable ownership after every transition—not merely requests emitted. A successful test must not hide a naked or oversized interval, duplicate action, or automatic release on an empty snapshot.
5. **Recovery and reporting.** For each ambiguous action specify how exact identity, all cumulative fills and terminality are established. If they cannot be established, report the unresolved state and protection uncertainty, retain ownership and halt conflicting automatic actions. A deliberate hold is a limitation, not a substitute for a functioning protective exit. Test that alerts and the phone snapshot distinguish unavailable, working, oversized and unprotected states.
6. **Independent gate.** A separate reviewer runs all witnesses against frozen bytes under Node 18.19.1, verifies the actual order adapter preserves tags and response fields, and completes the repository's Mac-box, restart-soak, trade-witness, health-alert, exact-install and rollback gates. All tests here are synthetic correctness work, not historical strategy performance studies. Provider behavior and account mode remain unverified until independently established; no candidate is installable before all applicable gates pass.
