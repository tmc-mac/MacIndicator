# Provider contract investigation — review only

The current strategy uses one entry with a full-size protective stop and separate partial and final targets. A target fill and stop fill can race before the client can react. If a partial exit has happened and an oversized stop cannot be canceled, a single remaining stop can itself reverse the position. The client cannot infer that an unanswered order or cancel will never execute from an empty open-order snapshot.

ProjectX documents [order placement](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/order/order-place/), including a unique account-wide `customTag`, optional stop and target brackets, and Auto OCO mode. Position Brackets is described as the default and does not accept those bracket objects. Rejection can still return an order ID; pending is a separate outcome. Search examples include `customTag`, but duplicate-tag rejection is not documented as an idempotent retry or a way to retrieve the earlier order. No account mode was queried or changed.

[TopstepX order management](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/14434175-topstepx) describes automatic cancellation of the other leg and per-entry OCO brackets. The public pages reviewed do not specify the guarantees needed for partial fills, simultaneous fills, parent/child identity, cancel/modify races, or exact stop and target price anchoring after entry slippage. [Order modify](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/order/order-modify/) supports size and stop price, but does not specify an atomic amendment guarantee. [Partial close](https://gateway.docs.projectx.com/docs/api-reference/positions/close-positions-partial/) documents an invalid size response when requested size exceeds the position; it does not specify atomicity against working stops or an idempotent retry contract.

One possible design is separate protected entry slices, but it changes the number and timing of entry orders and can change fill and slippage behavior. Relative tick brackets may also change the structural stop under slippage, depending on undocumented anchoring. It is therefore a design to investigate, not an implementation claimed equivalent to the current strategy. Obtain the provider's explicit contract and verify on an authorized independent rig before relying on it. No broker or live host was contacted.

The review driver records a unique custom tag and immutable request parameters before sending an order, preserves an uncertain dispatch across restart, and issues at most one cancellation per intent. When its saved plan has open inventory, it holds before sending a protective-stop cancellation; this preserves an actually working stop from that cancellation but does not prove the order still works or is the correct size. A terminal-looking cancellation record must match the exact order ID, account, contract and previously known fill quantity; even then the experiment retains the hold because the finality and completeness of fills under a cancellation race have not been established. Consequently ordinary stop changes or flattening can halt and a position may be unprotected or have an oversized stop. Urgent human intervention remains necessary.

The library-only `inspectHold()` reads evidence for a known order ID and `inspectHoldByTag()` can use an explicitly injected, read-only history-search adapter to find positive tag matches. The latter is not connected to the actual executor or broker adapter. Missing results, mismatched identity, multiple matching records, unavailable history or a tagless legacy record are not evidence of safety. Neither helper releases the hold, establishes terminality, proves a stop is active, or places a protective order.

After an unanswered close, two cases can look identical to the client: the close may never arrive, or it may arrive late after a replacement stop is placed. Sending the stop can therefore reverse an already-closed position; waiting can leave the original position unprotected. An empty snapshot cannot distinguish those cases. A safe automatic recovery requires a verified broker primitive or authoritative terminal identity and lifecycle evidence that rules out the delayed action. Until then, keep the ownership hold, surface the uncertainty and urgent need to inspect the position and all working orders, and do not blindly resend or automatically clear the hold.
